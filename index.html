Training failure is predictable, preventable, and repairable — without ever restarting.

SNR-SURGEON detects gradient signal-to-noise collapse along the sharpest loss directions steps before divergence, surgically damps only the unstable directions, and repairs Adam's poisoned moment buffers — rescuing the run with no checkpoint restart.

00 · The paper in one page

One central claim. Five components. Three kill criteria.

Headline: predict-and-repair without restart, saving X% of compute vs. checkpoint-restart on spike-prone LLM pretraining. Everything else in the paper exists to support that sentence.

C1 · DETECTOR

Directional gradient SNR

Tracked along the sharpest preconditioned-Hessian directions, estimated Hessian-free from microbatch disagreement. Collapses K steps before divergence.

C2 · THEORY

Guaranteed warning window

In the Adam-preconditioned quadratic regime, directional SNR provably decays polynomially before the stability threshold is crossed.

C3 · INTERVENTION

Surgical damping

Damp only the unstable eigendirections — explicit projected GD onto the stable subspace, preserving bulk descent.

C4 · REPAIR

Adam moment surgery

Fix the poisoned components of m and v directly instead of restoring a checkpoint and skipping batches.

C5 · CAUSAL VALIDATION

Random-direction control

Damping k random orthogonal directions at matched strength proves the identified directions — not damping per se — cause the rescue.

⚠ Kill criteria (test early)

Wk 4: surgical ≯ matched-strength global clipping → paper collapses.
Wk 5: lead time < 20–30 steps over λ_max tracking → "predict" claim is weak.
Moment surgery ≈ zeroing v → demote to ablation, not headline.

Background to master

Concepts checklist

  • Hessian of the loss, eigenvalues/eigenvectors; curvature λ and the GD stability threshold η·λ < 2
  • Edge of Stability (EoS): λ_max hovers at 2/η during training
  • Adam's update rule, bias correction, the preconditioner v_t, and the preconditioned stability threshold (~38/η in the adaptive-EoS analysis)

  • Hessian-vector products via double backprop / Pearlmutter's trick; power iteration & Lanczos for top-k eigenpairs
  • Loss spikes in LLM pretraining: causes (LR warmup, low precision, ε, data) and current fixes (skip batches, restart, clip)
  • Small-scale-proxies methodology (Wortsman et al.) — how to induce instability deliberately
  • Projected gradient descent and subspace methods

03 · Formula sheet

All the math

Setup
L(θ), Hessian H = ∇²L(θ), eigenpairs (λᵢ, uᵢ), λ₁ ≥ λ₂ ≥ … minibatch gradient g = ∇L(θ) + ξ, Cov(ξ) = Σ/|B|
Directional gradient SNR — the core quantity
SNR_u(t) = |E[gᵀu]| / √(Var(gᵀu)) = |∇L(θ)ᵀu| / √(uᵀΣu / |B|) # microbatch-disagreement estimator (near-zero overhead): # microbatch grads g₁…g_m already exist under gradient accumulation mean: ḡᵀu var: (1/(m−1)) Σⱼ (gⱼᵀu − ḡᵀu)² SNR̂_u = |ḡᵀu| / √(var̂/m) # cost: m dot-products per tracked direction per step — free
Directions to track — Adam's preconditioned eigenbasis
H̃ = D^(−1/2) H D^(−1/2), D = diag(√v_t + ε) # top-k of H̃ via power iteration / Lanczos with preconditioned HVPs # refresh every T_eig ∈ [50, 200] steps, warm-started from previous eigenvectors # HVP cost ≈ 2 extra backprops per refresh — profile and report it
Stability thresholds
GD: η·λᵢ > 2 → divergence along uᵢ GD + momentum: η·λᵢ > 2(1+β) Adam (adaptive EoS): preconditioned sharpness λ̃ hovers ≈ 38/η (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999 — derive cleanly in the appendix, don't just quote the constant)
Theorem 1 — the statement to prove
# quadratic loss L(θ)=½θᵀHθ, per-direction coordinate x_t = uᵢᵀθ_t, # SGD+momentum, then extend to frozen-preconditioner Adam approximation x_{t+1} = x_t − η(λᵢx_t + ε_t) + β(x_t − x_{t−1}), ε_t ~ noise, Var = σᵢ² # a stochastic linear recursion — AR(2) Let ρ(λᵢ,η,β) = spectral radius of the companion matrix. As λᵢ(t) sharpens toward λ* at rate α, i.e. λᵢ(t) ≈ λ* − α(T−t): • mean signal contracts like ρᵗ|x₀|, ρ → 1 • stationary noise variance amplifies like σᵢ²/(1−ρ²) ~ σᵢ²/(c·(λ*−λᵢ)) ⇒ SNR_uᵢ(t) = O(√(λ*−λᵢ(t))) — decays POLYNOMIALLY before the crossing ⇒ lead time T_lead ≥ f(α, σᵢ, detection threshold τ) # computable warning-window bound # Corollary (Theorem 2): surgical damping = projected GD onto # span{uᵢ : ηλᵢ < 2} — Damian et al.'s implicit self-stabilization, # made explicit, earlier, controllable. # Proof tools: AR(2) stationary variance, companion-matrix eigenvalues, # standard perturbation bounds. 2–3 weeks with mentor help.
The intervention — surgical damping
U = { i : SNR̂_uᵢ < τ and λ̃ᵢ > γ·λ* } g_damped = g − Σ_{i∈U} (1−cᵢ)(gᵀuᵢ)uᵢ, cᵢ ∈ [0,1] # cᵢ=0 is full projection; schedule cᵢ → 1 over R recovery steps
The repair — Adam moment surgery
m_t ← m_t − (m_tᵀuᵢ)uᵢ # remove poisoned first-moment component v_t ← along uᵢ: pre-spike EMA or robust median of recent uᵢᵀ(g⊙g) # required ablations: (a) full v reset (b) global vs directional surgery (c) no surgery # HEADLINE: wall-clock/FLOPs saved vs checkpoint-restart-and-skip (the PaLM recipe)
Metrics
  • Lead time — steps between detector trigger and divergence event (loss > μ + kσ of trailing window), vs. baselines: gradient-norm threshold, λ_max tracking, ZClip's z-score
  • Rescue rate — % of induced spikes where the run recovers to within δ of control loss within W steps
  • Compute saved — FLOPs(restart from checkpoint + reskip data) − FLOPs(surgery), as % of run
  • Detector precision/recall — ≥5 seeds per config, report CIs; false-positive rate on stable runs gets its own figure
  • Overhead — wall-clock % added by eigenvector refresh + SNR tracking

Spike-induction recipes (Wortsman + Molybog)

  • LR 2–5× above stable · short/no warmup · large batch + high LR combos
